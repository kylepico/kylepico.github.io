About Me

I earned my Bachelor of Arts in Information Science (Concentrations: Data Science and Information Ethics, Law and Policy).

My Perspectives as a Paralegal

At Cravath, Swaine & Moore LLP, I support a fantastic group of antitrust attorneys as a Senior Litigation Paralegal II. My work mainly revolves around filings under the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Antitrust Improvements Act of 1976. I’m a trusted resource at every stage of the HSR process, which consists of (1) a pre-signing stage, (2) the HSR filing, (3) the Waiting Period, and (4) the Second Request. This type of work is challenging, but very rewarding. It has given me new perspective on how integral legal work is in stimulating innovation. Additionally, on a daily basis, I demonstrate paralegal expertise in tasks pertaining to antitrust advisory, depositions, investigations, arbitrations, pro bono, and court filings at the district and appellate levels.

Besides legal work, my position as a senior member of the paralegal department presents me with numerous leadership opportunities. I train junior paralegals on how to balance multiple tasks simultaneously, how to effectively use Microsoft Excel, and how to uphold the high standard of professionalism and competence asked by Cravath. My time at this firm provides me an opportunity to assist high-profile clientele in their undertakings, and I’m grateful to play a role in the legal workflows. My time here ignites my passion for learning the law and fortifies my goal to become a lawyer.

Before Cravath, I was a Senior Litigation Paralegal at the United States Department of Justice, Criminal Division, Fraud Section. This position asked me to balance vast workflows of trial attorneys in matters related to white-collar fraud. I worked closely with these attorneys and federal law enforcement agents to ensure that the government presented their best arguments for the cases we handled. I supported numerous trial across the United States in this position and found myself to be an important resource for my attorneys, law enforcement agents, and fellow paralegals. In January 2025, I left this position in favor of the one at Cravath because I became interested in developing a drastically different perspective on the law, especially one that better serves my career interest in antitrust.

Combating Food Insecurity

I balanced my responsibilities as a Teaching Assistant with responsibilities dedicated to combatting campus food insecurity. From Spring 2021 to graduation, I was heavily involved in the Cornell Food Pantry where I earned the role of Senior Student Manager. I grew up on public school free/reduced lunch programs so it meant a lot to me to be involved in ensuring that Cornell’s students and faculty didn’t go to sleep hungry.

I furthered my interest in hunger relief as a Zero Hunger Intern for the Congressional Hunger Center in Summer 2022. In this capacity, I conducted research that was used in the first White House Conference on Hunger, Nutrition, and Health in over 50 years. I also worked in a team of other interns to present a policy proposal to Congressional Hunger Center’s corporate partners to improve efforts against food insecurity in the Mid-Atlantic region. This proposal centered on directing funds towards vertical farming practices in unoccupied real estate to provide fresh produce to those in marginalized communities.

I learned a lot about how policy decisions affect one’s access to affordable, healthy food options and I implemented the internship’s teachings in my final year of managing the Cornell Food Pantry. I became more vocal in my interactions with local groceries to include more nutritional food in their donations and connected with more food banks in the Tompkins County area to make sure the pantry’s inventory was diverse and well-stocked.
